######## home how the app works · ARMIES How the app works
Where every line of this app comes from, the tables it is kept in, and what is checked before any change reaches your phone.

1 · Where the data comes from
Your New Recruit lists; BSData, the data New Recruit is built from; Games Workshop’s Core Rules, Faction Packs and FAQs, and Wahapedia where GW has no document yet. Where they disagree, GW wins; where GW misprints (a typo, a base size), the correction is written down with its reason.
2 · How a list is built
A program reads the list’s New Recruit export and writes it into the tables: each unit an entry that points at its datasheet, its stats, weapons, keywords, points and rules text copied from the sources. A list with only a PDF is typed in, and a program checks it against the PDF line by line.
3 · The tables
Everything the app shows is in 14 tables in one file, data.js, drawn below with their real row counts and columns. Each column is tagged: mechanical, AI summary, AI reading or decided by hand.
4 · What checks every release
A second program compares every table with its sources again and renders every screen; if a single check fails, nothing is released. What it checks
5 · How the app draws pages
One page template reads the tables and draws every screen from them. Each release renders every screen of every list and compares its text with the last one accepted, so no screen changes unseen.

How they link: an army’s unit entries name their DATASHEETS row (ds) and the army names its FACTIONS row; datasheets name their rules in RULES, which RULE_ALIASES and RULE_SRC point at; GRANTS, RULE_OPTIONS and CHECKLIST point at a rule in FACTIONS or an ability of a datasheet; CORE_STRATAGEMS serve every list, BS_IDS the list import; CONCEPTS holds the core terms, VERSION the data’s version.

MECHANICAL copied by a program from GW, BSData, New Recruit or Wahapedia, or worked out from them by a program; the tag names the source.

AI SUMMARY a one-line gist of a stratagem, written by AI. It carries a fingerprint of the text it sums up: a change in GW’s words flags it to be read again.

AI READING the numbers and conditions behind the datasheets’ footnotes and the “When targeted” lines (not the Damage tab’s: its boxes are made by a program from GW’s words), read from GW’s words by AI (a few by a program): each carries a fingerprint of the words it was read from and is signed off by an independent reviewer.

HAND a decision about the rules written for this app, not copied from a source: when a rule is used (checked against what a program derives from its words) and which units it is for, notes, aliases; each signed off by an independent reviewer. Ids, short names and source labels are not decisions and are not signed.

Of the 555 columns of the tables: Mechanical 300 · AI summary 2 · AI reading 198 · Hand 55.

WHY YOU CAN TRUST THE DATA

Games Workshop is the source of truth. Where New Recruit, BSData or Wahapedia say something else, the difference is written down with its reason, and every release checks it again.

What AI wrote is marked on the tables above: summaries and readings. Each carries a fingerprint of the rule text it was written from, so a change in GW’s words stops the next release until it is read again. Every judgement the app adds, by AI or by hand, is signed off by an independent reviewer, and a changed rule voids its sign-off.

In the app, every summary written by AI carries a small AI label, and a tap on it opens the rule as written.

Nothing ships unless every check passes: the build refuses to run otherwise.

CORE RULE

Battle-shock

01.06 Leadership Rolls: To make a leadership roll for a unit, its controlling player rolls 2D6: if the result is equal to or greater than one or more of the Ld characteristics in that unit, that roll succeeds. Otherwise, that roll fails.

01.07 Battle-shock Rolls: To make a battle-shock roll for a unit, its controlling player makes a leadership roll for it (see above).

If that roll succeeds, that unit does not become battle-shocked.
If that roll fails, that unit, and each model in it, is battle-shocked.

While a unit is battle-shocked:

The Objective Control (OC) characteristic of all of its models is modified to ‘-’ (02.02).
Its controlling player cannot target that unit with stratagems (15).
It is not eligible to start an action (16), and any action it has started cannot be completed.

01.07.02 Battle-shock Test (app): The term Battle-shock test is the same as battle-shock roll.

08.03 Battle-shock: The active player must now make one battle-shock roll (01.07) for each unit in their army that fulfils one or both of the following conditions:

That unit is currently battle-shocked.
That unit is at, or below, half-strength.

If a unit was battle-shocked at the start of this step and its battle-shock roll during this step succeeds, it is no longer battle-shocked.

CORE RULE

Destroyed

Throughout a battle, models will suffer damage, lose wounds and be destroyed. When every model in a unit has been destroyed, that unit is destroyed.

When a model is destroyed, first resolve any rules that are triggered when it is destroyed, then it is removed from the battlefield. If any such rules apply, and if the model was destroyed as the result of an attack, unless otherwise stated, those rules are only resolved and that model is only removed after the attacking unit’s attacks have been resolved. Unless otherwise stated, destroyed models and units cannot use abilities or be selected or targeted by rules.

05.04.05 Fight On Death (app): Some rules enable models to attack after they have been destroyed. When a model under such an effect is destroyed, do not remove it from play.

Those models will stay on the battlefield until their unit is selected to attack and has attacked, or until the end of the phase (whichever comes first). Any rules triggered by those models being destroyed are resolved, and then those destroyed models are removed.

A model’s engagement range is the area of the battlefield within 2" horizontally and 5" vertically of it.

CORE RULE

Mortal Wounds

Some attacks or rules inflict mortal wounds on units. Each time a unit suffers one or more mortal wounds, its controlling player must resolve the following sequence for each of those mortal wounds, until either all of them have been inflicted or that unit is destroyed:

Select Model: Select one model in that unit by following the first instruction below that applies:
If a non-CHARACTER model in that unit has lost one or more wounds, you must select that model.
Otherwise, if that unit contains one or more non-CHARACTER models, you must select one of those models.
Otherwise, if one or more CHARACTER models in that unit have lost one or more wounds, you must select one of those models.
Otherwise, you must select one CHARACTER model in that unit.
Resolve Damage: The selected model loses 1 wound. If this reduces that model’s remaining wounds to 0, it is destroyed.

When resolving attack dice, if those attacks inflict a mixture of both mortal wounds and normal damage, resolve all of the normal damage first, then resolve all of the mortal wounds.

CORE RULE

Strategic Reserves

Before the battle, in the Declare Battle Formations step, you can select one or more friendly units (excluding FORTIFICATIONS) to place in strategic reserves. Instead of setting up these units on the battlefield during deployment, place them to one side; they are strategic reserves units, and will arrive later in the battle.

Unless otherwise stated, the combined points value of all of your strategic reserves units (including those embarked within TRANSPORTS that are themselves placed in strategic reserves) cannot exceed 50% of your points limit for your battle size.

20.01.01 Reserves (app): The term ‘Reserves’ is the same as strategic reserves.

20.02 Repositioned Units: Some rules allow units to be removed from the battlefield and placed in strategic reserves during the battle. Units that use such rules are known as repositioned units. In addition to any other rules that apply to such units (such as where they can or cannot arrive), all of the following rules apply to them:

If used in the Movement phase, such rules can be used on units that have already moved that phase.
A repositioned unit that is set up in the same turn in which it made an advance, fall-back or disembark move has still made an advance, fall-back or disembark move that turn.
When they are removed from the battlefield, any rules that are affecting such units for a specified duration or under specified circumstances continue to affect them while that duration and/or those circumstances apply.

20.03 Arriving from Strategic Reserves: To arrive on the battlefield, each strategic reserves unit must make an ingress move (see below). Unless otherwise stated, they can only do so from the second battle round onwards.

At the end of the third battle round, unless otherwise stated, all strategic reserves units that have not made one or more ingress moves are destroyed, with the following exceptions:

Units embarked within TRANSPORTS that have made an ingress move during the battle.
Repositioned units (see opposite).

CORE RULE

Engagement

A model’s engagement range is the area of the battlefield within 2" horizontally and 5" vertically of it.

While a friendly model is within engagement range of one or more enemy models, those models – and the units they belong to – are engaged with each other.
While a unit contains no engaged models, that unit is unengaged.

Wahapedia FAQ: Does my model or unit become engaged while moving through a enemy units engagement range during a move? No
